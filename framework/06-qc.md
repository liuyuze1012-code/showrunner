# 06 — QC: the checklist

> Machine-checkable things get checked by machine. Everything else gets read
> aloud by a human.

QC gates are never skipped for speed. Renders run in the background while the
next deliverable is authored — the checklist is not what makes a pipeline slow.

---

## A. Content and accuracy

1. **Asset mapping.** *View* every staged asset. Does its content match both
   its filename and the meaning of the line it covers? Mislabeled assets are
   the single most common silent failure.
2. **Name check.** Show, guest, company and product spellings verified against
   brand assets or the episode brief. **Never transcribe a name from audio.**
3. **Number check.** Every on-screen figure re-verified against the fact table
   from Gate B. ASR transposes digits, and a stat card repeats the error at
   full size.
4. **Frame audit at every graphic event.** Read every visible string and every
   number **aloud**. No clipped text, no overlaps, correct assets, sufficient
   contrast. Reading aloud catches what scanning does not.

## B. Motion and rhythm

5. **Motion audit.** Three-frame bursts at each entrance: it must actually
   move. Nothing static beyond 3s. No dead seconds.
6. **Rhythm audit.** No 10-second window without a beat.
7. **Junction audit.** ±3 frames at every A-roll cut: no wrong-face flash, no
   fade straddling a cut, no presenter flashing between two coverage elements.
8. **Speaker audit.** For every clip window, render a 1fps thumbnail strip and
   *read it*. The clip starts and ends on the intended speaker.
9. **Sync spot-check.** Three random element entrances against word timestamps
   from the final cut.

## C. Sources and compliance

10. **B-roll in-point audit.** Three sample frames per video asset **before**
    committing an in-point. Known traps: black fade-ins, color bars, slates,
    camera-label cards, blooper footage. For full-screen backing footage, also
    confirm nothing unintended is visible at the edges.
11. **AI labeling.** Every synthetic shot carries its chip; chip duration
    frame-matches the delivered clip; the platform declaration is in the
    delivery note.
12. **Attribution assembled.** Every amber and red row in `SOURCES.md` has its
    credit string in the description text.
13. **Persistent tags present** where required (e.g. "not investment advice")
    — frame 0 through the last frame, not just the relevant section.

## D. Audio

14. Voice normalized **before** effects are layered, never after the mix.
15. Peaks ≤ −1 dB. No clicks at splices.
16. Effects present, correctly sized, and varied — never the same sound on
    every event.

## E. Delivery probe

17. `ffprobe`: audio 48 kHz, constant frame rate, audio/video durations within
    0.2s, video DTS monotonically increasing.
18. **Never assemble by segment-cut plus stream-copy concat.** Always
    re-encode the concat. Stream-copy across segment boundaries produces
    progressive A/V drift that passes a spot-check and ruins the full runtime.
19. **Cache integrity.** For long-form built in sections: purge stale segments
    whenever content at an existing boundary changes. Same-duration stale
    segments are invisible to a file-size check.
20. **Subtitle pass.** Read the full subtitle track end to end. No duplicates
    from re-used hook lines, no overlapping cues, sentences complete.

---

## The subtitle cross-check

ASR output is a **draft**, never a deliverable.

1. Transcribe **per section of the final cut**, at segment level — never the
   whole file at once. Whole-file timestamps smear.
2. Apply a project-specific correction map for recurring model errors.
3. **Cross-check every cue against the approved script.** A delivered read is
   typically ~90% identical to the script; where they differ, the script's
   wording is almost always correct and the ASR is wrong.
4. **Number mismatches are escalated, not silently fixed.** If the presenter
   said a different figure than the script, that is an editorial decision.
5. Strip stray punctuation artifacts.

In the reference implementation this pass routinely catches 50+ errors per
episode that no spell-check would flag, because they are valid words in the
wrong place.

---

## Automate what you can

Machine-checkable, and therefore scripted rather than eyeballed:

- container/codec/sample-rate/frame-rate probes
- A/V duration match and DTS monotonicity
- subtitle overlap and duplicate detection
- static-frame detection (frame differencing) for the 3s rule
- asset reuse detection by hash

Everything else — meaning, taste, whether the image matches the claim —
requires a human or a careful agent pass. Automating the first list is what
buys attention for the second.
