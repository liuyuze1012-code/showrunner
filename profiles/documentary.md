# Profile — Documentary short

**Built from footage and interviews rather than argued from a script.** The
viewer leaves with an experience, not a summary. The slowest profile here, and
the one where restraint pays most.

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 8–30 min |
| Presenter | Usually absent, or voiceover only |
| Backbone | Narrative sequence |

## Structure

```
cold open   the strongest moment in the material, without setup
title       after the open, never before
body        chapters. each stands alone with its own hook and unresolved end
close       resolution, or an honest statement that there isn't one
```

**Chapters are the unit.** Each should survive being watched alone, and each
should end slightly open. The J-cut transitions below are how chapters connect.

## J-cut transitions

The technique that most separates documentary feel from explainer feel:

1. The chapter's final line finishes **on the speaker's face**
2. B-roll fades up over ~1.6s
3. **A full 2 seconds of silence** — 4s at a major break
4. The next chapter's first lines play as voiceover over B-roll
5. Return to face at the sentence boundary

**The silence is the technique.** Remove it and a documentary transition
becomes an ordinary cut. If the recording has no natural pause, split the
audio track and insert it in the edit.

## Trigger table preset

| # | Moment | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Establishing a place | Wide shot, held. **Longer than feels comfortable** |
| 2 | A subject speaks for the first time | Lower third, once |
| 3 | Historical reference | Archive footage, **era-matched** |
| 4 | A date matters | Date card, minimal |
| 5 | Describing something absent or unfilmable | The only acceptable use of generated imagery, labeled |
| 6 | Chapter break | J-cut with silence |
| 7 | Emotional peak | **Nothing.** Hold the shot |
| 8 | Contradicting sources | Split screen, both attributed |
| 9 | A figure that matters | Restrained stat card, then away |
| 10 | Resolution | Return to an image from the cold open |

Row 7 governs the genre. Every other profile decorates its peak; this one
holds the shot and gets out of the way.

Row 10 is the cheapest structural trick available: closing on an image from
the open makes twenty minutes feel composed rather than assembled.

## Gate emphasis

- **Gate C is the whole film.** Sequencing *is* the authorship. Expect several
  passes and don't rush them.
- **Gate B inverts.** The script often follows the assembly rather than
  preceding it. Write to what the footage actually says.
- **Gate E is narrow but legitimate.** Historical reenactment and unfilmable
  scenes only, always labeled — never to illustrate a documented event for
  which real footage exists.

## Compliance

- **Archive licensing is the main exposure.** Tier every asset and keep the
  source manifest from day one, not at delivery. See
  [`05-compliance.md`](../framework/05-compliance.md).
- **Era-match archive footage.** Using 1970s footage over a 1950s claim is a
  factual error, not a stylistic one.
- **Consent for interview subjects**, especially private individuals.
- **Never generate imagery of a real identifiable person** doing something
  they didn't do. Not labeled, not stylized, not as illustration.

## Failure modes

- **Cutting too fast.** The most common failure. Documentary pacing is slower
  than instinct, and the wide shot held two seconds past comfortable is
  usually the one people remember.
- **Scoring the emotion.** Music telling the viewer how to feel about a moment
  that was already working.
- **Voiceover explaining what's visible.** If the shot shows it, don't say it.
- **No unresolved thread.** Chapters that close completely give the viewer
  permission to stop.
