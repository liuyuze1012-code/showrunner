# Profile — Interview / podcast cutdown

**A long conversation becomes publishable pieces.** The material already
exists; the work is selection, not authorship.

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 60s shorts · 2 min topic cuts · 10–40 min episodes |
| Presenter | Host + guest |
| Backbone | Selection and sequencing |

## Three deliverables from one recording

| Format | Length | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Quick short** | ~60s | One striking segment. Minimal graphics — restraint *is* the style |
| **Topic cut** | ~2 min | One topic argued start to finish, host and guest woven, practical payoff |
| **Full episode** | 10–40 min | Conversation plays naturally, host voiceover bridges sections |

## Structure notes

- **Open on the guest's strongest claim**, not on an introduction. The face
  plus the claim *is* the hook; the name card can wait 20 seconds.
- **Credentials ride along with the claim.** Don't spend a separate segment
  establishing why the guest is worth hearing — attach it to the first thing
  they say that's worth hearing.
- **When the host quotes the guest, play the guest's tape instead.**
- **Over-include at the rough cut.** Keep whole exchanges. It's far faster to
  trim than to restore.

## Trigger table preset

| # | Speaker is… | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stating a surprising number | Stat card. Verify — ASR mangles figures in conversation |
| 2 | **Being introduced** | Name card. **Mandatory once per video** |
| 3 | Naming their company or product | Logo chip, during that segment only |
| 4 | Describing a transformation | Before/after |
| 5 | Landing a one-word answer | Big-word takeover |
| 6 | Describing something concrete ≥4s | B-roll or split screen |
| 7 | Listing out loud | Sequential visuals |
| 8 | Host bridging | Keep light — reuse 1 or 2 only if a number or name lands |
| 9 | Emphasizing a word | Accent keyword |
| 10 | **Telling a personal story** | **Nothing.** Clean face, slow zoom. The story is the show |

Row 10 matters more here than in any other profile. The instinct to decorate a
vulnerable moment is exactly wrong, and audiences feel the intrusion.

## Gate emphasis

- **Gate A belongs to the human.** The guest and topics are an editorial and
  relationship decision. Don't counter-propose unless asked.
- **Gate C is the real gate.** Selection *is* the edit.
- **Verify names obsessively.** Guest name, company, product, title — from a
  written brief, never from audio. Misspelling a guest's name is the one
  mistake that damages a relationship, not just a video.

## Failure modes

- **Cutting mid-thought** to fit a length target. Let the sentence finish; put
  the out-point past the final word.
- **Flashing the wrong face** at a cut. Render a thumbnail strip and read it —
  every clip must start *and* end on the intended speaker.
- **Over-cutting the host.** Removing every host line makes the guest sound
  like they're monologuing at no one.
- **Reusing hook footage without dedupe.** If the cold open reuses a line from
  the body, its subtitle appears twice.
