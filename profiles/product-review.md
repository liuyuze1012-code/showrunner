# Profile — Product review

**Evaluate a thing against criteria and reach a verdict.** The viewer is
deciding whether to spend money, so credibility rules are strict and
disclosure is legally significant.

| | |
|---|---|
| Length | 5–15 min |
| Presenter | On camera, with the product |
| Backbone | Criteria → evidence → verdict |

## Structure

```
0–10s    the verdict, or the single most surprising finding. Don't withhold it
~20s     who this product is for, and your test conditions
body     one criterion at a time: claim → your evidence → judgment
close    verdict, restated. Who should buy it, who shouldn't
```

**State the verdict early.** Withholding it to drive watch time is the oldest
trick in the genre and audiences are exhausted by it. The interesting part is
*why*, and people stay for reasoning.

## Trigger table preset

| # | Speaker is… | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stating a price** | **Price card. Always. With date and region** |
| 2 | Giving a spec or measurement | Stat card + how it was measured |
| 3 | Comparing to a competitor | Side-by-side, **same scale, same conditions** |
| 4 | Describing a physical feature | Close-up of *that* feature, not a general shot |
| 5 | Demonstrating | Uncut real-time footage. No speed ramps on a demo |
| 6 | Listing pros or cons | Sequential build |
| 7 | Giving the verdict | Full-screen verdict card |
| 8 | **Disclosing a sponsorship or free unit** | **Full-screen, unmissable, unskippable** |
| 9 | Emphasizing a word | Accent keyword |
| 10 | **Stating a personal preference** | **"Subjective" label.** Separate taste from measurement |

Rows 8 and 10 are the profile. Row 8 is a legal requirement in most
jurisdictions; row 10 is what stops a reviewer's taste from being read as a
test result.

## Gate emphasis

- **Gate B must lock criteria before testing.** Criteria chosen after you know
  the result is not a review, it's a rationalization.
- **A disclosure check belongs in Gate F**, as a blocking item.
- **Gate E is usually off.** Never use generated imagery of the product. Ever.
  A synthetic image of a real product is indistinguishable from a fabricated
  claim about it.

## Compliance

- **Disclose material connections** — free units, sponsorship, affiliate links.
  Spoken *and* on screen. This is FTC-regulated in the US and has equivalents
  in most markets.
- **Affiliate links disclosed in the description**, near the top.
- **Date and region every price.** Prices move; videos don't.
- **Never use manufacturer renders as your own footage.** Label them if used at all.

## Failure modes

- **Criteria drift** between products, making comparisons meaningless.
- **Hiding the sponsorship** in the description. If a viewer feels they found
  it rather than being told, you've lost more than the disclosure would have cost.
- **Speed-ramping a demo**, which destroys the evidence the demo existed to provide.
- **Reviewing the spec sheet** instead of the product. If your review could be
  written without touching the thing, it isn't a review.
